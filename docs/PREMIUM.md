# Steroids — Premium Feature Ideas

The entire core experience stays free. People pay for things that save serious time, help teams manage complexity, or remove infrastructure headaches.

The key: not charging for features competitors already give away free. Charge for the extra value built around them.

## 1. Premium features worth considering

### 1. Enterprise Fleet Control

Strong candidate.

Manage hundreds of agents across multiple PCs, servers and developers from one control panel.

- Centralized fleet management
- Machine health and agent availability
- Team-wide task distribution
- Resource usage and workload monitoring
- Policies applied across machines

Who pays: AI development teams, agencies and engineering departments.

### 2. Team Shared Brain Pro

A searchable knowledge system that learns from completed work and helps agents reuse team knowledge.

- Shared project knowledge
- Search across past agent sessions
- Reusable workflows and lessons
- Team-wide skill libraries
- Permission-controlled knowledge sharing

Who pays: Teams that repeat similar coding tasks across projects.

### 3. Autonomous Workflow Automation

Let users create workflows that coordinate agents automatically.

- Run tests after code changes
- Assign tasks to specialist agents
- Trigger workflows when a GitHub PR opens
- Retry failed tasks according to user-defined rules
- Run scheduled maintenance tasks

Who pays: Developers who want less manual orchestration.

### 4. Security and Governance

Give companies the controls they need before allowing agents to operate across their machines.

- Centralized approval policies
- Role-based permissions
- Audit logs and activity exports
- Secrets and credential protection
- Organization-wide agent restrictions

Who pays: Businesses with sensitive code, customer data or compliance requirements.

### 5. Agent Analytics and Optimization

Help users understand which agents and workflows are actually worth running.

- Cost per task and project
- Success and failure rates
- Time saved estimates
- Model and agent comparisons
- Bottleneck and repeated-failure reports

Who pays: Heavy AI users and teams managing model expenses.

### 6. Managed Remote Access

Keep away-from-home control free when self-hosted, but sell an optional managed service that makes it effortless.

- Secure remote connection setup
- Relay infrastructure
- Device pairing and management
- Reliable reconnection
- Managed notification delivery

Who pays: People who want remote access without configuring their own infrastructure.

## 3. What should actually be premium?

Ranking:

### 01 — Fleet management for teams

The strongest business-oriented option. As users add machines and agents, centralized control becomes more valuable.

### 02 — Team knowledge and shared workflows

The more a team uses Steroids, the more useful its accumulated knowledge and reusable workflows become.

### 03 — Automation and integrations

Customers pay for reliable workflows that remove repeated manual work, not merely for a dashboard.

### 04 — Managed infrastructure

A natural recurring-revenue opportunity because operating relays, notifications and hosted services has ongoing costs.

### 05 — Enterprise security and administration

Fewer customers may need this, but organizations can pay for controls, auditability and centralized management.

## 4. A possible pricing model

Starting hypotheses to test, not verified market prices.

### Community — R0

Individual developers.

The full free feature list: Lens, router, phone access, remote access, previews, approvals, notifications, voice and screenshots, fleet view, continuity and shared brain.

### Pro — R99–R199/mo

Advanced personal productivity.

Advanced automation, powerful workflow creation, extended analytics, integrations and optional managed services.

### Team — R499–R1,499/mo

Agencies and small engineering teams.

Shared knowledge, collaboration, centralized policies, team administration and multi-machine management.

### Enterprise — Custom

Larger organizations.

Advanced security, deployment support, organization-wide governance, custom integrations and service agreements.

One important rule: if the core project is open source, avoid making the community version artificially crippled. Keep self-hosting and the promised free features free. Charge for genuinely additional business capabilities and services.

Biggest recommendation: build Steroids as the free control center for AI agents, then monetize team-scale automation, knowledge, governance and managed infrastructure. Those are stronger long-term business opportunities than charging users just to access their own agents.
